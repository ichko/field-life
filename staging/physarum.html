What this is

Physarum — Sage Jenson's pictures, Jones' 2010 rule — with the particles thrown away, the way Field Life throws away the dots of particle life. Normally it is a few million agents with a floating-point position and a heading, each smelling the trail at three points ahead, turning towards the strongest, stepping forward and leaving a little trail behind. Here there are no agents. There is a grid, and every cell holds eight numbers: how much of it is currently heading east, north-east, north, and so on round the compass.

That count is the whole trick, and it is worth saying why. The obvious way to put a swarm on a grid is to keep a density and a single direction per cell — one arrow, one number. It does not work. Two streams that meet head-on average their two directions into nothing and annihilate; the field breaks into isolated blobs within a few dozen steps. Real agents pass straight through each other, because each one carries its own heading and nothing averages them. Eight channels per cell is the smallest thing that can do the same. Two crossing streams live in two different channels and never touch.

One step

The eight directions are the eight neighbours, so a heading is one of eight and a ±45° turn is exactly one channel across. That coincidence is what makes the whole thing cheap and exact. A step is four full-screen draws:

— Smell. Twenty-four bilinear taps of the trail: for each of the eight headings, the value sense cells ahead and the values sense cells out at ±angle either side of it. These are the three sensors of Jones' agent, computed once for every heading a cell might hold rather than once per agent.

— Turn. Each channel j splits its contents three ways — into j−1, j, j+1 — in proportion to exp(sharp·smell) at the corresponding sensor. Push sharp up and it is Jones' hard "turn towards the biggest"; pull it down and the population hedges. The three shares sum to one, so this step moves mass between channels and creates none.

— Move. Channel i is shifted one cell along direction i. An integer shift — no interpolation, so no numerical diffusion, and nothing is lost or gained. This is the part that a warp with bilinear sampling gets wrong: resampling is a blur, and a field transported that way smears itself flat within a hundred steps.

— Trail. Every cell adds deposit times what it is carrying to the trail field, which is then blurred by diffuse and multiplied by decay. Networks are the standing balance between that reinforcement and that decay, and almost every knob worth turning is on one side or the other of it.

Mass is conserved to the bit. The readout under the field measures it on the GPU by summing the grid, and it does not move off its opening value however hard the picture is driven — the same guarantee, and for the same reason, as the transport on the flat page: every share that leaves a cell arrives somewhere.

The knobs

knobwhat it does
sensehow far ahead the sensors sit. Sets the size of everything — the mesh is about this across
anglehow wide the two side sensors splay. Narrow makes long strands, wide makes cells and chambers
sharphow decisively the turn is taken. Low is a fog, high is Jones' argmax and crisp filament
crowdhow much a cell is repelled by an occupied destination. Jones' collision rule, softened. Zero lets veins collapse to points
diaga penalty on the four diagonal channels, which move √2 per step where the others move 1. Around 0.3 hides the lattice
jitternoise added to each decision. A little keeps the network exploring; a lot boils it
deposit / decay / diffusethe trail's side of the balance. Long decay and small diffuse give thin hard veins
fillhow much mass a reseed lays down. It never changes afterwards, so this is the one setting that is really about the whole run

Handling it

Drag to pan, wheel to zoom, shift-drag to paint mass in and alt-drag to take it out — painting is the one thing that changes the total, and the readout will show it. Space holds it, R reseeds, N rolls a new random set of knobs, H hides the panel, F is fullscreen. The view picker swaps the trail for the mass itself, or for the heading — hue by which of the eight channels is winning — which is the quickest way to see why the eight are needed: the veins are two-toned, opposite colours running through each other.

This is a staging page — a test of whether the physarum rule survives having its agents removed, not a finished thing.
