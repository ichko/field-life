What a cell is

Twelve numbers. Four are density: how much of each of four fluids sits in the cell. Eight are velocity, a vector for each fluid, because the four do not have to be going the same way. Pressure is worked out fresh every step, one number per fluid, and is not kept. That is the whole state — twice that under the trained rule below, which has four more fluids you cannot see. Everything a cell learns, it learns from the cell next door.

One step

— Smooth. Each density is blurred by a small binomial stencil, range times over. The blurred copy is what the other fluids feel: a few passes of a local blur is the same as one wider kernel, so the reach of the interaction is a slider without any cell ever reading further than two neighbours out.

— Pressure. For fluid c in a cell: Uc = stiff·ργ − g·Σd M[c][d]·ρ̃d. The first term is an equation of state on the total density, the same for every fluid — it is what stops anything piling up, and gamma is how hard it says no. The second is the matrix: fluid c is pulled towards wherever the smoothed density of d is high when M[c][d] is positive, and shoved off it when negative. U is high wherever the fluid does not want to be.

— Push. Velocity takes −dt·∇U, a central difference on the cell's four neighbours. Then the four fluids in a cell drag on one another, each relaxing towards the cell's mass-weighted mean by couple; neighbouring cells drag by visc; drag bleeds a little off everything; and a cap keeps every speed under one cell a step, which is what keeps the next part honest.

— Move. This is the MaCE rule. Each fluid in each cell splits its mass among its nine neighbours, itself included, giving neighbour k a share proportional to exp(Ak), where the affinity is

Ak = −|ok − v|² / 2·spread²  −  seep·(Uk − Uhere)

The first term is a Gaussian sat on the cell's own velocity, read off at the nine offsets: mass goes where the velocity carries it, spread by spread. The second leans the shares towards lower pressure directly, without waiting for the velocity to get there. The nine shares are normalised at the source, so they sum to exactly one, so every unit that leaves a cell arrives somewhere. The total of each fluid does not change for the life of the run; the readout at the bottom measures it on the GPU. The velocity rides along: what a cell holds after the move is the mass-weighted mean of what arrived, so momentum is carried, not just mass.

The matrix

Row c, column d: how much fluid c wants to be where d is. Drag a cell up or down to set it; double-click to zero it. The matrix need not be symmetric — red can chase green while green flees red — and that is where the chasing and the orbiting come from, exactly as in particle life. The diagonal is a fluid's opinion of itself: positive and it beads into drops, negative and it spreads thin. Two fluids that dislike each other do not mix, and the boundary between them is a real interface, held by pressure on one side and the matrix on the other.

The trained one

The preset called gecko takes the matrix away and puts a small network in its place. Each cell reads, for each of eight fluids, the density, its two Sobel gradients and its Laplacian, and the velocity — forty-eight numbers — through one hidden layer of sixty-four, and answers with a potential and a push per fluid. The equation of state is still added on top, the velocity still takes the gradient, and the move is still the same nine shares, so it is the same fluid with a different opinion about where to go. Four of the eight fluids are hidden: never drawn, but moved by the same rule and conserved the same way, and they are the rule's memory — a marker that has spread this far says how far the edge is.

It was fitted, in train/fluid_gecko.py, so that a disc nine cells across grows into the lizard emoji and then holds. Mass is conserved here, so the disc has to arrive already holding what the picture costs, colour by colour, plus an amount of each hidden fluid that the training chose. The fitting ran the very step the page runs, written out in JAX, with a pool of half-grown geckos so that holding still is learned along with growing; the page reproduces that step to the last rounding. The paint view is the one it was fitted to: each fluid its colour, added.

What is kept and what is not

Mass is exact, per fluid, at 32-bit — the shares are computed by the same arithmetic at every one of the nine cells that use them, so what the destinations add up to is what the source had, to the last rounding. At 16-bit the storage rounds and the readout will drift in the fourth place. Momentum is not conserved and is not meant to be: drag and coupling are there to take it away, and the pressure force does work.

What this is not is incompressible. There is no projection step, no Poisson solve; a stiff equation of state stands in for the constraint, and a hard shove sends a ripple rather than an instantaneous rearrangement. And a nine-way softmax is a diffusion: narrow spread and the mass stays put until the velocity is a good fraction of a cell, wide spread and everything smears. That knob is the whole trade, and it is the same trade as on every page here.

Handling it

Drag to pan, wheel to zoom. Shift-drag paints the fluid picked under paint (or a random one), ctrl-drag stirs, alt-drag erases; the same three are under drag in the panel for a trackpad. Space holds, R reseeds, N rolls a new matrix, 1–4 pick the fluid to paint, H hides the panel, F is fullscreen. Link puts the whole world — matrix, scalars, seed — in the address bar, and the sweep is a shelf of such links.
