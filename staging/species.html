Why this one exists

On the flow page the eight kinds all move by the same nine shares, worked out once from the cell's heading. Locked together like that they can only ever blend, and they do: a purity of 0.15 was the best anywhere in a sweep of fourteen runs, where 1 would be one clean kind to a cell. No amount of pushing them apart afterwards wins against being transported together.

Here each kind gets its own affinity and its own nine shares, which is what Field Life does. Red goes one way and green another because they want different things, so they come apart on their own rather than being held apart by force.

One step

— Look around. Each of the eight fields is read on three rings — at near, mid and far — six taps to a ring. Every kind weighs those three rings differently, so every kind is really looking through its own kernel: one wants a close huddle, another wants a shell at arm's length.

— Want. The matrix mixes them. M[c][d] is how hard kind c is pulled towards what its rings saw of kind d, minus a term for how crowded it already is. That leaves one number per cell per kind: how much kind c wants to be there. The matrix need not be symmetric, and the asymmetry is where chasing comes from.

— Move. Each kind hands its contents to its nine neighbours in shares proportional to exp(beta·A) at each of them, scaled to sum to one. Eight kinds, eight sets of shares, all independent. Nothing is created and nothing is lost, for each kind separately.

It is written the way the flat page writes it — E = exp(beta·A), Z = sum of E over the nine, S = rho/Z, rho' = E·(sum of S) — because the share from q to p is Ep/Zq, which factorises. That is what keeps eight independent transports down to one number per cell per kind instead of nine.

The rings are turned

The three rings are sampled at angles measured from the cell's own heading, and facing says how much heavier the front of the ring counts than the back. At zero the kernel is a plain disc and this is Field Life with a matrix; wind it up and each cell reads the world in front of itself, which is the physarum sensor, and the two behaviours are then the same term rather than two terms bolted together.

The heading is not a rule of its own. It is read back out of the transport — a cell ends up facing whichever way its contents actually went — so it costs nothing and cannot disagree with the motion.

Reading it

species gives each kind a hue and mixes them by how much of each is present, so a cell that is one clean kind is a saturated colour and a cell that is an even blend of all eight is grey. The colour says purity, which is the thing being asked about. The readout under the field measures every kind's total on the GPU; none of them moves off its opening value.
